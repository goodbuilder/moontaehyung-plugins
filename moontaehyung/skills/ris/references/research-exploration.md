# Research exploration and milestone recording

Use the [unified self-critical workflow](self-critical-workflow.md) to select and revisit
perspectives and route observed failures. This reference retains ownership of all 12 breadth
checks and the complete return checklist below; selecting perspectives does not waive them.

1. Read project context and existing evidence; summarize what is known, inferred and still needed.
2. Ask about desired results/success criteria only where materially unresolved. Research direction,
   acceptable tradeoffs and confirmed choices remain user decisions.
3. Survey primary sources, including recent work and necessary foundational work. Browse for current
   trends. Record actual date window, queries/source channels, retrieval date, inclusion limits,
   source identity/locator and whether abstract, full text, code or results were inspected.
4. Group approaches by mechanism/problem addressed. Compare recent changes, representative evidence,
   limitations and open questions. Separate publication observations from claims of field-wide trends;
   limited access/search failure never proves an empty research gap. Check preprint/published versions.
5. Link trend findings to outcome elements and a breadth inventory of distinct mechanisms: current
   baselines, established and emerging approaches, relevant hybrids and resource-constrained routes.
   Search for missing alternatives and counter-evidence before narrowing; no fixed option cap.
6. Invoke Project Plan's recursive expansion protocol on every viable family. Investigate successive
   data, method, integration, evaluation and fallback choices where relevant. Forecast unselected
   options conditionally; do not wait for user selection to explore their consequences. A comparable
   report or one experiment proposal does not complete this work. Keep deeper questions differentiated
   by the parent choice instead of giving every branch the same generic "collect data and test" leaf.
7. Record breadth coverage, examined/excluded families, evidence limitations, and per-option frontier
   reasons with continuation actions. Stop only at genuine evidence/intent/constraint boundaries or
   a route specified through consequential downstream choices. Prioritize remaining work explicitly.
8. Project Plan owns decomposition, forecasts and graph records. RIS owns source notes and project
   context. Reuse canonical artifacts and stable IDs instead of creating competing reports.

## Explore causes and solutions together

Start with the outcome. Identify the phenomenon, system boundary, inputs, outputs, internal state,
environment and desired behavior that matter to it. Trace how an observation was obtained, including
what was measured, inferred, assumed, modeled or computed and what actual state remains unobservable.
Check the supplied problem explanation against the evidence while preserving the user's goal and
constraints. Ask for a value judgment or permission only when the affected work needs it.

Work from both ends as soon as there is enough context to act. For a decision-sensitive phenomenon:

- Follow a plausible cause through the variables it changes to the observed effect. Investigate
  upstream causes, interactions, common causes and competing explanations where they could change
  the decision. State the applicable conditions, supporting and contrary observations and the
  counterfactual prediction if the cause changed. Quantify sensitivity, magnitude or a bound when
  possible. Record what measurement or comparison could distinguish the explanations. A correlation
  or a model fit can motivate a hypothesis even when the mechanism remains unidentified.
- Use the cause to search for interventions at different points. An option might remove a cause,
  reduce its frequency or size, interrupt transmission, change sensitivity, compensate, bypass,
  detect or recover. Pursue viable suboptions, complements and prerequisites under the breadth
  discipline below. An observed benefit can justify investigating a solution while its mechanism
  remains hypothetical.
- Investigate each consequential option in reverse. What must be true for it to work, which
  variable or path does it affect, and what newly exposed cause or constraint could defeat it?
  As soon as the answer could change a candidate, give the follow-up its own question in the
  active frontier, with the discovery that triggered it and the decision it could reverse. Pick
  evidence that would discriminate between the predictions. Open the relevant passage, compare a
  previously unexamined condition, or run the calculation now. If the evidence is already in hand,
  use it for this new test. Return what changed to the originating branch while it can still act.
  Do this also for verification results and new constraints, including inconclusive results.

Leave an inspectable chain in the existing finding, study, plan or conversation: the triggering
observation and locator → follow-up question and parent ID → investigation actually performed and
its result → revised assessment or candidate ID. Include the earlier judgment and what still holds.
Attach applicable work-log IDs to the question. A retrospective explanation or a proposed
investigation leaves that link open. When a check is blocked, give its prerequisite and reopening
condition. Continue independent source and alternative searches meanwhile.

Depth depends on the decision. Use probabilistic or interacting explanations when warranted. Apply
RCA to an observed failure, FTA to a defined combination of failure conditions, or RMEA to
prospective risks when those methods answer the question. Ordinary factual lookups can finish
without a causal investigation.

## Run the next investigation before closing the branch

For an ongoing multi-stage investigation, use the existing plan or working context
to track consequential frontier questions by branch. Once the first-level
questions are established, take the next available research action in each viable
second-level or deeper branch. Keep exploring independent alternatives while a
user answer is pending. Prioritize actions that can eliminate a mechanism,
change the recommended bundle, or alter its verification.

For competing next actions, state which possible result would change which choice, what
the check needs, and whether it can be done now. Among equally discriminating checks,
prefer one with lower known cost or delay. Keep unknown costs explicit. If research was
requested and an accessible original log could settle the question, inspect it before
proposing another experiment. Compare the observed metric and conditions with the actual
requirement. Check that the observation belongs to the candidate in this case. If the
user or record establishes that link, use it. If it is ambiguous, report the measured
value and leave the candidate judgment open. Keep independent cases and their locators
separate. Report measurements, calculations, simulations and proposed tests separately.
If a necessary check is unavailable, give its reopening condition and continue independent work.

- **Ready:** Name the next source, calculation, or authorized check. Execute it
  in the current run and record the result and its effect on the branch. If the
  result exposes a consequential child question, add it to the frontier.
- **Conditional:** If a prerequisite has not been observed but its plausible
  outcomes can be bounded, investigate the resulting branches under labeled
  assumptions. Keep the branch ready while a useful independent action remains.
- **Blocked:** Record the exact evidence, user choice, or permission missing,
  which conclusion it prevents, and what would reopen the branch. Continue work
  elsewhere. An unselected candidate or one failed search does not block an
  otherwise investigable branch.
- **Resolved or out of scope:** Record the evidence that settled the question
  or the concrete constraint that excludes it.

Before closing a research run or issuing a final recommendation, review the
frontier against the outcome. Which unresolved explanation, intervention or interaction could still
change the choice? Weigh the expected decision value of the next discriminating action against its
cost and the agreed scope. Preserve the breadth checks below and record any consequential residual
uncertainty. Use alternate search terms and follow new leads as the breadth protocol requires.
Stop repeating the same check when its inputs, method and decision value have stayed the same. If a consequential ready action remains and the
user asked to carry out research, execute it and review the frontier again.
For a timed investigation, keep the current recommendation, supporting locator and unresolved
frontier ready to return as evidence arrives. Recheck the clock before starting another branch
or expanding the plan. Leave time to incorporate the review and deliver the answer. At a budget
or scope boundary, report the remaining ready action and provisional status. A generated map may be shared
as a progress artifact. Rendering and graph checks do not close ready branches.

### Deliver within an explicit time limit

At intake read the host clock and set a delivery checkpoint before the user's deadline. Allow for
observed answer and tool latency. As soon as the investigation yields a decision-bearing result,
deliver a short provisional answer to the user in the conversation before starting another
assignment, editing the plan or rendering an artifact. Include the finding, its supporting
locator or calculation, unresolved decision-changing work, and the actual audit status.
A progress announcement or a private note alone cannot serve as that answer.

At the checkpoint, issue the scoped final answer with dispositions for the required breadth,
frontier and assurance checks. If a check is unfinished, report the partial result and reopening
condition. Before the checkpoint start or await another check only if the remaining time also
allows delivery. Preserve any existing requirement for independent acceptance.

## Exploration breadth discipline

Domain-neutral checks to apply before ranking approaches, so the candidate inventory is not limited
to the first familiar family. Items 3-12 are required deliverables in addition to any output format
the request specifies.

1. Decompose the goal into unknowns; for each decisive one list every kind of source: direct
   measurement, derivation from resources already at hand, something newly constructed or added,
   and estimation from the process itself. Make these source kinds the first split under that
   unknown, before any single approach is expanded: open every one as its own branch (write
   "no candidate found" for an empty one). After listing candidates, state the premise all of
   them share and add a branch that negates that premise.
   When an option works only at one operating setting of a resource, re-open it at every other setting the conditions allow and state what that would require, instead of leaving a setting unexamined because the option was first built at another.
2. Read each given condition at its stated scope only; enumerate what stays free. Reject an option
   only for a stated constraint or quantified evidence, not an assumed rule of thumb.
3. List every named product/standard/tool/dataset in the request (if it names none, say so and
   skip this item), then look up each one (variants, versions, interfaces, specs) in primary sources; an entry without a lookup is listed as unresolved. Show the results as a per-entry table.
   When search tools are available, "not searched" is not an acceptable final state for a named
   entry: run at least one search per entry that names its specific variant or version, and
   search again for every figure you use in an estimate unless it comes from a
   source you actually read. A figure from memory must be labeled as such and queried before it
   decides a ranking.
4. Search before ranking: run and record several distinct source searches, including how the same
   unknown is handled in practice and adjacent fields. If search tools are listed as deferred, load
   them first. "Bounded" is allowed only when search is unavailable, not as a substitute.
5. Run a second gap pass after the first inventory; revisit options rejected on assumption.
6. Output a fragment-combination table (which partial results from different families together
   settle each decisive unknown) and list unknowns still open.
7. For every option dismissed as "insufficient alone" or "cannot do X", state the minimal
   complement (what to add, and how it would combine with the existing elements) that would
   make it sufficient, and test that pairing before keeping the dismissal.
8. Build a resource-by-unknown matrix. Rows: every resource the request gives or allows (named
   resources and quantities, quantities already known or obtainable, anything that may be added or
   built). Columns: each decisive unknown. In each cell ask what that resource, alone or paired
   with another, could do beyond its stated role, and vary how it is used or arranged when
   asking. Fill every cell with a candidate or a
   reason it is empty; do not leave a resource used only in its default role without saying so.
9. A candidate that meets the stated goal is not the end of exploration. After one does, counter-search for materially different principles or resources. Account for
   credible alternatives within the stated constraints before ranking. When a quantitative model rates an option borderline, first
   vary the assumptions the model made and
   recompute before rejecting it; reject only on the recomputed figure.
10. Depth per branch: before writing "not found", "no data" or "unresolved" for any item, run at
    least two differently worded queries or open a primary source; one failed search is not
    evidence of absence. Follow each lead that a result raises with a further query before moving
    on, so queries depend on earlier results instead of following a fixed topic list.
11. Read, do not skim: for each of the top candidate options and each figure that decides a
    ranking, open the primary source text (not only a search snippet) and note what was read.
12. Work the frontier: before returning, take the most consequential items from your own list of
    unexplored areas and open questions and investigate them (at least the top three, or all if
    fewer); return only what remains after that. For several independent approach families, give
    each its own bounded investigation (use the delegation guidance below when it is available)
    so one family's depth does not depend on the others.
    Items 3-12 are required deliverables in addition to any output format the request specifies.

### Return checklist

Verify before returning; do the missing item now, not later: (a) a per-entry
lookup table for every named product/standard/tool in the request, each with a search that names
its variant or version; (b) at least three distinct source searches recorded; (c) a fragment-combination
table; (d) for each option dismissed as insufficient alone, the minimal complement and how it
combines; (e) every dismissed option still listed with its reason; (f) the resource-by-unknown
matrix; (g) a counter-search for materially different alternatives after the first goal-meeting option; (h) for each borderline option, the recomputation under varied assumptions; (i) every
"not found"/"unresolved" item backed by two differently worded queries or a primary source;
(j) primary text actually opened for the top candidates and ranking-deciding figures; (k) the top
unexplored items investigated, with the remaining frontier listed.

## Recording events
- Goal: record the user's confirmed desired outcome, success criteria and constraints.
- Decision: record explicit selection, reason, authority, conditions and review trigger; preserve the
  previous event and supersession link. Presenting alternatives is not a selection event.
- Verification: record methods, conditions, version, observations, limitations and relevant forecasts.
  An agent can establish a measured result without claiming that the user accepted the choice.
- Save provisional forecasts as part of an authorized plan/map artifact; label them provisional.
  This does not authorize logging all exploratory dialogue or collecting private history.
- Update INDEX/CONTEXT links and active plan at these milestones; record current node and next action.
  Resume from the current source bytes, not an old HTML file. A graph revision is not a new research
  project and does not change project_id or plan_id.

### Apply a correction to the decisions that used it

Read the previous and corrected evidence at their locators. First establish whether the
new observation supersedes the previous one or was taken under different conditions.
Follow every dependent claim and candidate, including those that share the source.
Reassess the affected recommendations, identify what independently still holds, and name
the observation that could change the remaining decision. A correction already reflected
in the current evidence adds no new decision event. An inconclusive correction can withdraw
a recommendation without proving the opposite result.

Within the authorized recording scope, keep the existing decision ID. For an ordinary decision,
update the current recommendation and its reason in the canonical plan and CONTEXT, retaining the
prior recommendation and its evidence in history. For a managed study, update its canonical study
record instead; the plan and CONTEXT link to it rather than duplicating its changing assessment or
recommendation. A user's previous choice remains attributed to them. Explain why changed evidence
calls for reconsideration. Apply the usual expected-hash and preimage checks to managed notes, reread
concurrent human edits and report partial updates. Conversation-only requests receive the
reassessment in the answer.

## Multi-session studies

Use this workflow only for a study the user requested or authorized; ordinary research does not
need a separate study record, and the default recording policy remains manual. For prior study
observations, reuse logs from the same project. Reuse an external study observation only when the
user explicitly selects the source and observation; retain its locator, provenance and conditions.

For competing explanations of the same observation, state what each assumes and record structured
predictions for each planned test under named conditions. Show which possible results would
strengthen or weaken each explanation, which fail to distinguish them, and which changed conditions
would make the test inapplicable. Choose the next test for its ability to change a consequential
decision among the remaining explanations; if its outcomes would not change the next step, defer it.

When a later visit returns with a raw result log, reopen the current canonical study and compare the
result with its predictions. Check the actual protocol, configuration, baseline, target, source or
other conditions that could change applicability. Classify which explanations the result strengthens
or weakens, or whether it leaves them unresolved; a changed or missing condition can make the result
inconclusive or inapplicable, and absence of a result is not negative evidence. Keep observation
separate from interpretation and identify the next discriminating step. When authorized, add the raw
observation and interpretation to the canonical study through its guarded update path, with a link to
the result log and actual conditions; otherwise report without persisting. A study recommendation
remains provisional; neither a result nor a recommendation is a user selection. New evidence can
revise the recommendation without changing a recorded user choice. Record a new selection only on
the user's explicit choice; link its authoritative source when carrying an existing choice forward.

Use the [shared output contract](shared-outputs.md#managed-studies) for the canonical record and
links. Read the [study workflow](study-workflow.md) for its guarded update commands.

## Candidate overlap is valid

When assembling research candidates, reuse the same decision/option reference in every applicable
candidate. Do not assign a method, dataset, sensor or validation choice to only one candidate, or
remove it from another candidate after it has been used once. Project Plan owns candidate bundles
and candidate-local single/multiple selection rules. Compare commonalities as well as differences;
record each candidate independently and preserve its identity when selections change. A hypothetical
candidate is not a confirmed user decision. Keep the full bundles in the canonical plan for resume.

## Completion and recommendation checks

Scale these checks to the request; a narrow factual answer does not require a project survey.

- Establish coverage from the outcome: required capabilities, inputs/resources, interfaces,
  operating conditions and evaluation. Derive domain-specific elements rather than reuse a
  fixed component list. Before narrowing, account for consequential alternatives and exclusions.
- For each consequential unresolved item, identify whether it is investigable now, requires a
  user preference/authorization, or needs unavailable external evidence/equipment. Investigate
  available items that could change the recommendation before returning the choice to the user.
  Continue independent work while a necessary answer is pending.
- Check a recommended bundle end to end, including dependencies and shared resource budgets.
  Distinguish identified configurations from allocations, hypothetical bundles and sensitivity
  scenarios. A parameter sweep does not establish that every row is an executable configuration.
- Disclosing an assumption does not resolve it. Investigate or bound decision-sensitive assumptions;
  show how the choice changes across plausible bounds. If a decisive uncertainty cannot be resolved,
  give a conditional recommendation and the specific evidence needed, not an unconditional ranking.
- Separate unavailable experiments from available investigation, calculations, source inspection
  and preparation. Do not treat inability to validate the entire system as inability to advance it.
- Before ending, compare the deliverable with the active completion boundary and remaining work.
  Finish useful authorized investigation that remains. End when that scope is satisfied, or explain
  a concrete evidence, intent, permission or resource boundary and its effect. Do not end merely
  because a first table, record, next-step list or experiment proposal has been produced.
- Avoid unbounded exploration: prioritize uncertainties that can change feasibility or preference;
  defer low-impact branches with reasons. Do not require every possible combination to be tested,
  purchases made, or user choices finalized to complete a bounded research request.
- Report what is established, conditional and unperformed. Where recording is authorized, update
  the current plan/state so a resumed agent can identify the goal, remaining work and blockers
  without reconciling a chain of contradictory append-only updates. For a managed study, link to
  its canonical record for the current assessment and recommendation rather than copying them.

### Evidence behind a recommendation

Before calling an eligibility condition unknown, read the current decision entry and
the applicable observation. For each condition that affects the answer, distinguish
“must be satisfied” from “already satisfied”. If the two sources have different scope,
give both bounded statements in the answer or decision record: what the existing decision
says, with its locator, and what the observation independently establishes, with its
locator. A terse older note marking a condition satisfied establishes its reported
status, even if the evidence for independently verifying it was not supplied. Keep
that distinction when carrying a user selection into CONTEXT or a handoff. For example, a log about
processing time leaves a separately accepted training qualification in force.
Reconsider an established condition when the evidence or scope that supported it changes.

For a claim that could change a choice, link the wording to the passage actually inspected
and the experiment, dataset or observation it describes. Distinguish direct reporting,
restatements and your own inference. Give the conditions that carry over to the user's
case, the relevant counterevidence and what remains unknown. If a source cites an
unavailable original, identify what you read and what you could not check.

Count independent support at the level of the claim. Several papers may reuse one
sample, and one paper may report multiple independent experiments. Establish that
relationship from the methods or provenance. Titles, DOIs and read counts alone leave it unresolved.
When the relationship is unclear, keep independence unknown. Keep query/read counts in
the existing work log and interpretation in the answer or an authorized finding.

For a recommendation, map each stated requirement to its supporting passage or decision record.
Check that measurements apply to the candidate, revision, site and operating conditions wherever
these affect the choice. Carry forward an in-scope decision when its owner records it or the user explicitly confirms
that the responsible party approved it. Attribute a reported approval to that record. A site mention or a measurement taken there leaves approval open.

When a decisive condition is unresolved, identify the smallest available check that would settle
it. For example, look up a run’s site alias before applying its timing result to the requested room.
Use the run timestamp to find the room’s humidity when the user requires that condition. If the
available records leave a gap, report the supported findings and make the recommendation conditional
on the missing fact. Once the evidence and applicable decisions settle the stated choice, return
the recommendation and its scope. An incidental variable warrants more searching only when it
could change that choice.
